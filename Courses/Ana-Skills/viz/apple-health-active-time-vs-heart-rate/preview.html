More active-tagged time didn’t mean a higher heart rate

Across the 10 fully-sampled days in this export, days with more heart-rate readings tagged “active” are not meaningfully higher in average heart rate than quieter days. The relationship is weak (r ≈ 0.23) and close to flat.

Takeaway: More active-tagged minutes in a day does not track with a higher average heart rate in this window — the strongest reading of the data is the absence of a relationship, not the presence of one.

Scope: This export contains only heart-rate readings, no Workout or ActiveEnergyBurned data, and covers 11 days in January 2020 — not a full year. “Active-tagged hours” is Apple’s coarse motion-context flag on background heart-rate sampling, not a logged exercise duration. Two partial-coverage days (Jan 16, Jan 27) are excluded from the comparison.

Source: Apple Health export, parsed and loaded to BigQuery (ai-map-agent-489008.apple_health.heartrate); full query and pipeline in Courses/Ana/.
