# 4.4 | Organizing Files with Claude Cowork

## Learning Objectives

- Master the complete method for using Cowork to handle real, messy file-organization tasks;
- Be able to design clear categorization rules and have the AI execute them reliably;
- Complete a real file-organization task and produce a standardized archive structure.

## Suggested Duration

25 minutes (in-depth hands-on)

## Prerequisites

- Have completed 4.1–4.3, and understand basic Cowork operations and how to judge applicable scenarios.

## Course Outline

### I. Common Pain Points in File-Organization Tasks

- Large numbers of files, chaotic naming, and lack of unified categorization standards;
- Manual organization is time-consuming, error-prone, and inconsistent;
- Goal: let the AI take on the repetitive labor of "identifying content + suggesting categories + executing the archive," while you are responsible for gatekeeping the categorization standards and final confirmation.

### II. Designing Clear Categorization Rules (a Key Prerequisite Step)

Before issuing the task, think through three questions clearly — they directly determine the quality of the organization:

1. **What are the categorization dimensions?** By file type? By project? By date? By importance level? (Can be a combination of multiple dimensions)
2. **How fine-grained should the categories be?** For example, should "contracts" be further split into "pending signature," "signed," and "archived"?
3. **What is the naming convention?** Does file renaming need to follow a unified format (such as `date-type-brief description`)?

> Instructor tip: this step is the real core of this lesson. Many learners skip the design step and just throw "help me organize this" at the AI, resulting in uncontrollable output. Emphasize that "the more clearly you've thought through the categorization rules, the more accurately the AI will execute."

### III. Hands-On: Walk Through the Full File-Organization Process

1. Prepare a batch of real/sample messy files (15–30 recommended, covering several different types);
2. Create a new task in Cowork, clearly describing the categorization rules:

```
> Please help me organize this batch of files, categorizing according to the following rules:
> 1. First split into: documents, spreadsheets, images, other, based on file type
> 2. Further subdivide the documents category into subfolders by topic
> 3. Rename all files uniformly to a "date-brief description" format; use the date mentioned within the file content, or keep the original filename if the date cannot be determined
> Please give me a categorization plan first, and I will confirm it before you execute
```

3. Review the categorization plan given by the AI (echoing the safety habit of Plan Mode from 1.5), checking whether files with ambiguous categorization or that require human judgment have been reasonably flagged;
4. After confirming, execute, and observe the actual archiving results;
5. For parts of the categorization that aren't ideal, give direct corrective feedback: "these files should be categorized as XX, not YY," and observe whether the AI adjusts correctly and applies the same logic to similar files.

### IV. Handling Ambiguous/Edge Cases

- Some files naturally belong to multiple categories (e.g., a document that is "both a contract and involves financial data") — agree on a handling principle with the AI in advance (e.g., "categorize primarily by main purpose, and note the secondary category in the filename");
- For files the AI cannot judge, require it to explicitly list a "needs human confirmation" checklist rather than guessing arbitrarily — this is also a continuation of safe usage habits (better to ask too much than to silently make an error).

### V. Output Verification

Once organizing is complete, ask the AI to self-check in one sentence:

```
> Please check the folder structure after organizing, confirm the file count in each subfolder, and list any files whose categorization you are not certain is correct
```

This step has the AI perform its own "quality check," further reducing the cost of manual review.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: There are an especially large number of files (hundreds or thousands) — can Cowork handle that?**
A: Due to context window limitations (learned in 2.6), there is a practical upper limit to the number of files a single task can process. For very large batches, it's recommended to process them in batches, or combine with the automation scripts from Chapter 5 for rule-based batch processing.

**Q: Could the AI accidentally delete files?**
A: Organizing and categorizing generally only involves moving/renaming, not deletion. Operations involving deletion follow the permission-confirmation mechanism (echoing the safety thread from Chapter 1) — be sure to carefully review the categorization plan before execution.
