# 4.1 | Cowork Workflow Basics

## Learning Objectives

- Review and reinforce the initial understanding of Cowork from 1.2, moving into systematic, operational-level mastery;
- Understand the basic shape of the Cowork workflow: creating a task, scoping the data, and alternating between human and AI;
- Complete a full hands-on run through the Cowork task lifecycle (from creation to completion).

## Suggested Duration

20 minutes

## Prerequisites

- Have completed 1.2 (Basic use of Cowork and Code on Claude Desktop).

## Course Outline

### I. Review: What Is Cowork

- Quickly review the core takeaway from 1.2: Cowork is the workspace within Claude Desktop designed for "multi-file, multi-step collaborative tasks," distinct from single-turn Q&A, and also distinct from Projects, which function as a long-term knowledge base;
- This lesson shifts the focus from "getting to know" to "operating proficiently."

### II. The Basic Lifecycle of a Cowork Workflow

```
Create task → Scope the data → AI breaks down the execution plan → Step-by-step execution (human/AI alternation and confirmation) → Produce output → Archive/export
```

Step-by-step explanation:

1. **Create the task**: Describe the goal in one clear sentence, rather than a vague "help me look at these files";
2. **Scope the data**: Upload or link the files/data involved in this task, making it explicit that "this workspace only processes this content";
3. **AI breaks down the execution plan**: Similar to Claude Code's Plan Mode (learned in 1.5), Cowork will also first present a task breakdown for you to confirm;
4. **Step-by-step execution**: After processing part of the task, the AI may pause to check with you on key decision points, rather than completing everything in one go;
5. **Produce output**: Presented in the form of documents, spreadsheets, Artifacts web pages, etc.;
6. **Archive/export**: Decide whether the output of this task should be saved into the Project knowledge base, or exported as a local file.

### III. Hands-On: Walk Through the Full Task Lifecycle

Give learners a lightweight task to follow along with in real time:

1. Open Claude Desktop and create a new Cowork task;
2. Task description: "Help me organize these 5 documents, categorize them by topic, and write a one-sentence summary for each";
3. Upload 5 sample documents (the sample material pack provided with the course can be used);
4. Observe the execution plan given by the AI, and confirm or adjust it;
5. Follow along with the execution process, noting at which points the AI proactively asks for your judgment;
6. Review the final output and discuss whether it met expectations.

### IV. Key Differences Between Cowork and Regular Conversation (Class Discussion)

Pose the question: "If this task didn't use Cowork, and you just told the AI in a regular chat box in one sentence to process 5 files, what would be different?"

Guide the class toward this conclusion:
- Regular conversation is better suited to single, simple, one-off Q&A;
- Cowork is better suited to work that has "clear task boundaries, requires multi-step processing, and may require your judgment to intervene during the process." Its value lies in **the manageability of the task and the controllability of the process**, not merely the surface-level ability to "process multiple files."

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can a Cowork task be paused midway and resumed later?**
A: Generally, you can resume a previous task and continue working on it. The specific save/resume mechanism depends on the current product version — it's recommended to test this before class to confirm.

**Q: How many files can a single Cowork task handle?**
A: There is no strict "hard cap" on the number of files that is worth teaching learners as a fixed rule, but due to the context window mechanics (learned in 2.6), the larger the number/size of files, the more processing quality and speed may be affected. It's recommended that a single task focus on one clear goal rather than piling on unlimited material.
