# 4.6 | AI Market Research and Competitive Analysis

## Learning Objectives

- Master the workflow for using Cowork to complete systematic market research and competitive analysis;
- Distinguish this lesson from 3.3/3.4 (research from a content-creation perspective) — this lesson targets the more general scenario of product/business competitive analysis;
- Complete a structured competitive analysis report.

## Suggested Duration

30 minutes (in-depth hands-on)

## Prerequisites

- Have completed 4.1–4.5; it is recommended to have already studied 3.3 and 3.4 (the research and benchmarking methodology can be directly carried over and reused).

## Course Outline

### I. The Relationship Between This Lesson and the Research Lessons in Chapter 3

- 3.3/3.4 focus on the "content creation" perspective — finding topics, deconstructing viral content;
- This lesson focuses on the "product/business" perspective — competitor positioning, pricing, features, and market strategy, better suited for product managers, business leads, and entrepreneurs;
- The underlying methodology is the same (gather information → organize it into structure → distill insights); this lesson's focus is on how to organize a more formal, more systematic analysis within the Cowork environment.

### II. Framework Dimensions for Competitive Analysis

Give learners a directly applicable analysis framework:

| Dimension | Analysis Focus |
|---|---|
| Product positioning | Target users, core selling points, differentiation claims |
| Feature comparison | Core feature list, unique features, missing features |
| Pricing strategy | Price tiers, payment model, discount strategy |
| Content and marketing | Main acquisition channels, content style, distribution tactics |
| User feedback | Positive and negative points from public reviews |
| Recent developments | New feature releases, funding/partnership news, and other important updates |

### III. Hands-On: Complete a Competitive Analysis with Cowork

**Step 1: Create the Task and Define the Data Scope**

Create a new task in Cowork, with a clear goal:

```
> I need to do a competitive analysis of [Product/Brand A, B, C]. Please systematically organize the information for me across five dimensions: product positioning, feature comparison, pricing strategy, content and marketing, and user feedback
```

**Step 2: Gather Information in Batches**

Guide Claude to research/organize dimension by dimension, avoiding stuffing too many requirements into one go, which leads to shallow coverage of information:

```
> Focus first on the "pricing strategy" dimension — help me find the current pricing page information for these three competitors, and organize it into a comparison table
```

**Step 3: Cross-Verify Key Data**

For key factual data such as pricing and core features, require the source and retrieval time to be noted:

```
> Please annotate each data point in the table with its source link and the approximate time you retrieved it, so I can verify it later
```

**Step 4: Distill Insights**

```
> Based on the above information, what are the core strengths and potential weaknesses of each of these three competitors compared to our product? Please give a well-reasoned analysis, not generalities
```

### IV. Compiling the Competitive Analysis Report

Consolidate everything into `docs/Competitive-Analysis-Report-[date].md`:

```markdown
# Competitive Analysis Report

## Subjects and Scope of Analysis
## Product Positioning Comparison
## Feature Comparison Table
## Pricing Strategy Comparison
## Content and Marketing Tactics
## User Feedback Summary
## Overall Insights and Recommendations
```

### V. Reminders on Information Timeliness and Verification

- Competitor information (especially pricing and features) changes quickly, and the information the AI retrieves may be out of date — be sure to manually verify the latest status before important decisions;
- When it comes to public review-type information, maintain an objective retelling and avoid making unverified negative claims.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can the AI directly tell me a competitor's internal data (such as real sales figures or costs)?**
A: No. The AI can only analyze and infer based on publicly available information. Non-public internal business data needs to be obtained through other legitimate research channels — AI analysis should clearly distinguish between "known facts" and "reasonable inference."

**Q: How long does an analysis report need to be to be considered sufficient?**
A: Use "can it support an actual decision" as the standard, rather than pursuing length. A five-dimension framework backed by real data is usually more valuable than a report that piles up a lot of text but lacks substantive information.
