# Chapter 4 | Data Research, Knowledge Base, and Office Collaboration

## Chapter Positioning

Chapter 3 focused on the vertical scenario of "content creation." Chapter 4 expands the scope to the more general **office collaboration and data research scenario**. The protagonist shifts from Claude Code CLI back to the Cowork workspace in Claude Desktop (an initial impression was already established in 1.2), aimed at learners who need to handle large volumes of files, spreadsheets, data research, and cross-platform collaboration (roles such as operations, administration, product, and market research).

After completing this chapter, learners should be able to:

1. Systematically master the foundational operations and core feature modules of the Cowork workflow;
2. Identify the typical office scenarios that Cowork is best suited to solve;
3. Use Cowork to complete practical tasks such as file organization, spreadsheet data processing, market research, and competitive analysis;
4. Connect the collaboration pathway between Claude and common office tools/knowledge bases such as Feishu, Google Sheets, Notion, and Google Drive;
5. Package repetitive collaboration workflows into Cowork Skills, achieving a complete workflow triggered by a single shortcut;
6. Build a reusable library of Cowork prompts and an AI collaboration SOP checklist, serving as an everyday office efficiency toolkit.

This chapter shares the methodological core of "data organization and research analysis" with Chapter 3, but differs in tool form and application scenarios — Chapter 3 is "creation-oriented," while Chapter 4 is "efficiency-oriented." The two chapters can cross-reference each other's hands-on experience.

## Learner Prerequisites

- Have completed Chapters 1–2 and are familiar with the basic Claude Desktop interface and Projects configuration;
- Recommended preparation: a batch of real files/spreadsheets that need organizing (even a messy desktop folder is fine), a Feishu or Google account (for the 4.7 exercise), a Notion account (for the 4.10 exercise), and background information on a product/project that needs competitive analysis (for the 4.6 exercise).

## Chapter Deliverables / Evaluation Criteria

After completing this chapter, learners should produce:

- [ ] At least one completed real-world file organization task, with a clearly categorized result;
- [ ] One AI-processed structured spreadsheet (any of: cleaning, categorization, or aggregation);
- [ ] One market research/competitive analysis report;
- [ ] A demonstrated connection between Claude and at least two external collaboration tools (choose any two of Feishu, Google Sheets, Notion, Google Drive);
- [ ] At least one packaged Cowork Skill, successfully triggered with one click via a shortcut/phrase;
- [ ] A personal list of frequently used Cowork prompts (at least 8 entries) and an AI collaboration SOP checklist (at least 1 complete task workflow).

## Instructor Notes

- This chapter is highly hands-on; it is recommended to reserve "follow-along" time in every lesson rather than pure lecturing;
- 4.7 and 4.10 involve third-party platform authorization and connections. Confirm the specific connection method for the current version in advance (this may be via MCP, a Plugin, or an official platform integration, depending on current product capabilities) — be sure to test the full process once before class;
- 4.9 involves the specific configuration entry points and shortcut trigger methods for Skills/Plugins, which will also change as Claude Desktop versions iterate — verify the operation path against the latest version before class;
- For learners working with real company data, remind them to anonymize sensitive data; classroom demonstrations should prioritize sample/anonymized data.
