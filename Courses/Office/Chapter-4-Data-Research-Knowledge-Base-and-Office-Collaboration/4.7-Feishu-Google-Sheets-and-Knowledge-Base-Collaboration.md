## Course Outline

### I. Why We Need to Connect to External Collaboration Tools

- Pain point: if the results of research and organization only ever stay inside the Claude chat box, team collaboration still requires manually moving them into everyday office systems (Feishu documents, Google Sheets), creating information fragmentation and duplicated work;
- Solution: through officially supported connection methods (possibly based on the MCP protocol, echoing 2.4 and Chapter 5), let Claude directly read and write content within these external systems, closing the last gap between "analysis" and "landing in collaboration."

> Instructor tip: the specific connection methods and the range of supported platforms will change as the product is updated. The core of this lesson is to clearly explain "the significance and basic usage posture of connecting" — the instructor needs to verify the actual connection method supported by the current version before class (e.g., via an official integration entry point, or via MCP Server configuration), and update the hands-on steps accordingly.

### II. Authorization and Safety Reminders Before Connecting

- Connecting an external account involves authorization, which usually requires you to log into the corresponding platform account and explicitly agree to the scope of authorization;
- **Principle of least privilege**: prioritize "read-only" or "scope-limited" authorization (if the platform supports granular control), rather than broadly granting full access;
- After authorizing, periodically check the list of connected apps, and revoke connections that are no longer in use — this is an extension of the "Five Principles of Key Security" from Chapter 1.

### III. Hands-On: Connecting Feishu Document Collaboration (Sample Workflow, Verify Against Current Version)

1. In Claude Desktop, find the settings entry point related to integrations/connections;
2. Select Feishu, and follow the prompts to complete account authorization;
3. Once authorization is complete, test the connection:

```
> Please help me create a new document in Feishu, titled "[Test Document]", with content being the summary of the market research report we just organized
```

4. Go to Feishu to confirm whether the document was created successfully and whether the content is complete and accurate.

### IV. Hands-On: Connecting Google Sheets Collaboration

1. Complete Google account authorization and connection;
2. Test syncing the spreadsheet data organized in Lesson 4.5:

```
> Please write the sales data we just cleaned and aggregated into the "Sales Summary" spreadsheet in my Google Sheets
```

3. Go to Google Sheets to confirm whether the data was written accurately, and whether the formatting is as expected.

### V. Knowledge Base Collaboration: Making a Project a Shared Team Asset

- Revisit the Claude Project created in 2.3, and discuss: in a team collaboration scenario, how should the knowledge base data within a Project stay in sync with the team's everyday Feishu knowledge base/shared documents, to avoid the problem of "one copy on the AI side, another copy on the team side" causing information inconsistency;
- Recommended practice: periodically (e.g., weekly) sync the core data accumulated in the Project to an updated copy in a team shared document, forming a habit of dual-track maintenance ("AI side + team side"), until automatic cross-tool syncing capabilities mature further in the future.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: After connecting an external tool, can the AI see all the historical data in my account?**
A: It depends on the scope of authorization and the specific task instructions. Generally, the AI will only read/write within the scope you explicitly request, and will not proactively browse through unrelated historical data. The specific granularity of permissions depends on what the platform supports — it's recommended to test and confirm the boundaries before class.

**Q: What should I check if a connection fails or authorization behaves abnormally?**
A: Common causes include insufficient account permissions, an enterprise Feishu/Google Workspace administrator restricting third-party app authorization, or network environment restrictions. It's recommended to first confirm your account permissions, then contact your enterprise IT administrator to check whether there are relevant restriction policies.
