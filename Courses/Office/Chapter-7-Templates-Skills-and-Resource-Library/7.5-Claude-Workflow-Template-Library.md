# 7.5 | Claude Workflow Template Library

## Learning Objectives

- Understand the official course-provided workflow template library, as a supplement and source of inspiration for your personal resource library;
- Master how to "port" a general-purpose template to your own specific scenario;
- Complete at least one exercise of template porting and actual use.

## Suggested Duration

25 minutes (case walkthrough + hands-on)

## Prerequisite Knowledge

- Have completed 7.4, and already have the basic skeleton of a personal AI resource library; this lesson builds on that foundation by adding external template resources.

## Course Outline

### I. Personal Resource Library vs. Official/Community Workflow Template Library

- What 7.4 built was a resource library that "grew organically out of your own hands-on experience," naturally fitting your genuine needs, but with limited coverage;
- The "Claude Workflow Template Library" introduced in this lesson is a **collection of general-purpose workflow templates** consolidated by the course team, covering a much broader range of common scenarios, and can serve as an important supplementary source for your resource library;
- The relationship between the two: the official template library is like a "public library," while your personal resource library is like "your own bookshelf" — when you need something, first go to the public library for reference, then borrow what fits and adapt it into a commonly used tool on your own shelf.

### III. Hands-On: Template Porting Methodology

Give learners a "three-step porting method":

**Step 1: Find a Matching Template**

Browse the course resource library and find the template closest to your own needs (it doesn't need to be a 100% match — a "70% similar" one will do).

**Step 2: Have Claude Help Adapt and Rework It**

```
> Here is a general-purpose [template name] template: @template-file.md
> Please help me adapt it into a version suited to my actual situation
> (combined with [add your specific background information]),
> reworking it into a version that fits how I'll use it
```

**Step 3: Actually Use It and Consolidate It Back into Your Personal Resource Library**

Once reworked and verified to actually work well through real use, archive it into your personal AI resource library following the method from 7.4, completing the closed loop of "public template → personal customization → personal asset."

### IV. Hands-On Practice

Have learners select 1 template on the spot (it's recommended to choose something related to the direction of your practice in previous chapters, such as content creation or office collaboration) and walk through the full three-step porting method.

### V. Contributing Back: Sharing Your Templates (Encouraged, Not Required)

- Echoing the source logic of the "50 Skills list" in 7.1 — a good template library needs continuous contribution from the community;
- Learners are encouraged: if you've polished a particularly useful Skill or template in 7.3 or 7.4, feel free to organize it and share it back to the course community, forming a positive cycle of "learn — create — share."

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What should I do if the official template conflicts with a method I've worked out myself?**
A: Go with whatever method you've actually verified works. The template library provides "general-purpose starting points" and "sources of inspiration," not standard answers you must strictly follow — your real, verified usage experience always takes priority over a general-purpose template.

**Q: How often is the template library updated?**
A: The course team maintains and updates it continuously. It's recommended to check back periodically (for example, whenever you retake the course or get a version update notice) to see whether any useful new templates have been added.
