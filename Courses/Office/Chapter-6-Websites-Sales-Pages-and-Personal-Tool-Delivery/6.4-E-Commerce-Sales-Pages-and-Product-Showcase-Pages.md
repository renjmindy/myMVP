# 6.4｜E-Commerce Sales Pages and Product Showcase Pages

## Learning Objectives

- Understand the structural differences in emphasis between physical-goods e-commerce pages and digital product sales pages (6.3);
- Master the core elements of a product showcase page: multi-image display, spec parameters, and purchase-decision support information;
- Complete the development of an e-commerce product showcase page, applying the data/table skills learned in Chapter 4 to handle product parameter information.

## Suggested Duration

30 minutes (In-depth Hands-on)

## Prerequisite Knowledge

- Completed 6.1 and 6.3, understanding the Brief breakdown method and sales page conversion structure. This lesson focuses on elements unique to e-commerce scenarios.

## Course Outline

### I. Differences Between E-Commerce Product Pages and Digital Product Sales Pages

| Dimension | Digital Product Sales Page (6.3) | E-Commerce Product Showcase Page (this lesson) |
|---|---|---|
| Key Decision Factor | Value alignment, pain-point match | Physical product details, trust (material/size/shipping/after-sales) |
| Visual Focus | Primarily copy and conversion structure | High-quality, multi-angle images are central |
| Additional Information | FAQ, social proof | Spec parameter table, shipping policy, after-sales guarantee |

### II. Core Sections of an E-Commerce Product Showcase Page

```
1. Main product image carousel: multi-angle photos, lifestyle/usage-scenario images
2. Core selling points summary: iconographic presentation of 3–5 key selling points
3. Product detail images and copy: detailed introduction of material, craftsmanship, usage method, etc.
4. Spec parameter table: structured information such as size, color, material
5. User reviews/customer photos (if real data is available)
6. Shipping and after-sales policy
7. Purchase action area: spec selection, quantity, add to cart/buy now
8. Related/recommended products (optional)
```

### III. Hands-On: Structuring the Spec Parameter Table

This is where this lesson directly connects with the knowledge from Chapter 4 — products often have multiple SKUs (combinations of color, size), which is well-suited to table-based thinking:

**Step 1: Organize Product Parameters**

```
> I have this product spec information [provide raw data such as color, size,
> corresponding prices], please help me organize it into a structured spec parameter
> table for use on the product page.
```

**Step 2: Convert into Interactive Page Elements**

```
> Based on this spec parameter table, please build an interactive spec-selection
> component for the page (e.g., clicking a different color swatch automatically
> updates the corresponding main image and price).
```

### IV. Hands-On: Visualizing Product Selling Points

- In e-commerce contexts, "images paired with text" is more persuasive than plain text alone;
- Drawing on the design philosophy of the course's `dataviz` skill (consistent visual language, clear information hierarchy), turn selling points into concise icon + short-phrase combinations rather than long blocks of text:

```
> Please turn these three core selling points: [Selling Point 1], [Selling Point 2],
> [Selling Point 3], into an icon + short-phrase card-style presentation, following
> the visual guidelines in @docs/Visual-Design-System.md.
```

### V. Building Trust Elements with Integrity

- Shipping and after-sales policies must be truthful and accurate — don't promise service terms you can't deliver just to boost conversion;
- If user reviews/customer photos are real data, they can be displayed appropriately; if there's no real data yet, don't fabricate it — instead build trust with real, verifiable information such as "quality guarantee/craftsmanship details" (echoing the compliance reminder from 6.3).

### VI. Checking the Mobile Shopping Experience

- Mobile traffic typically makes up a large share of e-commerce visits, so be sure to thoroughly check image loading, spec-selection interactions, and the tappability of purchase buttons on mobile;

```
> Please check whether the purchase flow on this product page is smooth on mobile,
> and whether the spec selection and purchase button are easy to tap.
```

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What if the product photos aren't professional enough?**
A: This lesson focuses on page structure and the development workflow — preparing professional photography assets is prerequisite work done beforehand. At minimum, ensure the images are clear and show the product's key details; thoughtful layout and design can partially compensate for lower-quality source material.

**Q: Will a multi-SKU product page be especially complex to build?**
A: The key is to first organize the parameter data properly (echoing Step 1, "structured processing"). Once the data structure is clear, having Claude generate the corresponding interactive components is usually smoother than expected — this is also why this lesson emphasizes "organize the data first, then build the page."
