# Chapter 6｜Websites, Sales Pages, and Personal Tool Delivery

## Chapter Positioning

Chapter 3 established a content creation system, and Chapter 5 connected up automation and tool integration capabilities. Chapter 6 brings these two threads together into a concrete "deliverable": **using Claude Code to turn an idea into a real, live website or tool that you can show, use, or sell to others**.

After completing this chapter, learners should be able to:

1. Break down a vague website/landing page idea into an actionable development requirements brief;
2. Independently produce a personal brand website for content creators;
3. Independently produce a sales page for an AI digital product;
4. Independently produce a sales page/product showcase page for an e-commerce scenario;
5. Deploy the automation scripts written in Chapter 5 to the cloud (using Vercel Cron as an example), achieving true 24/7 unattended execution.

This chapter is the watershed moment from "practice" to "delivery" — the output of the first 5 chapters mostly remained at the level of local files, conversation logs, and personal use. The goal of Chapter 6 is to produce results that **can be published directly to the public**, directly reusing the personal content context and visual design system accumulated in Chapter 3.

## Suggested Lesson Schedule

| Lesson | Title | Suggested Duration | Format |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6.1 | Landing Page Requirements Breakdown: From Idea to Actionable Brief | 25 minutes | Lecture + Hands-on |
| 6.2 | Personal Brand Website Workflow for Content Creators | 35 minutes | In-depth Hands-on |
| 6.3 | AI Digital Product Sales Page Workflow | 35 minutes | In-depth Hands-on |
| 6.4 | E-Commerce Sales Pages and Product Showcase Pages | 30 minutes | In-depth Hands-on |
| 6.5 | Moving to the Cloud: Using Vercel Cron to Run Scripts 24/7 Automatically | 30 minutes | In-depth Hands-on |

The total duration of the chapter is about 2.6 hours. It is recommended to split it into 2–3 class sessions: 6.1 should be a standalone warm-up, 6.2–6.4 can be taught as one selected topic or all three (depending on learners' actual focus), and 6.5 wraps up the chapter independently.

## Learner Prerequisites

- Completed Chapter 3 (personal content context, visual design system) and Chapter 5 (scripting, MCP, scheduled automation);
- It is recommended that learners already have a clear delivery goal in mind (a personal brand website? a digital product sales page? or an e-commerce product page?) and have decided in advance which lesson to focus on for in-depth practice;
- An account usable for deployment (such as a Vercel account) is needed for the 6.5 hands-on session.

## Chapter Deliverables / Success Criteria

After completing this chapter, learners should produce:

- [ ] A complete Landing Page requirements brief document;
- [ ] At least one page from 6.2/6.3/6.4 actually developed and previewed locally;
- [ ] One real cloud deployment completed, resulting in a publicly accessible URL;
- [ ] One scheduled cloud task deployment of a script completed, verifying that it can run automatically without human supervision.

## Instructor Notes

- This chapter delivers the strongest sense of accomplishment in the entire course. Instructors should reserve ample time for live demonstrations and peer review to boost learners' sense of achievement and motivation;
- Deployment involves real domains and real accounts, so instructors should remind learners to protect personal information, and prioritize using test accounts for classroom demonstrations;
- The cloud deployment in 6.5 is an "irreversible and publicly visible" operation. Following the safety-confirmation habits emphasized repeatedly in earlier chapters, make sure learners triple-check the content before deployment.
