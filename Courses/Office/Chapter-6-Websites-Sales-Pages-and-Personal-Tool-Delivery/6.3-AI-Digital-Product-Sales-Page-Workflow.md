# 6.3｜AI Digital Product Sales Page Workflow

- Complete the development of a sales page for an AI digital product;
- Understand the fundamental difference in design goals between a sales page and the brand website from 6.2 — conversion takes priority over showcasing.

## Prerequisite Knowledge

- Completed 6.1 (Brief); it is recommended to have completed 6.2 or at least understand its workflow, as this lesson reuses the same development method while focusing on the structural differences unique to sales pages.

## Course Outline

### I. The Fundamental Difference Between a Sales Page and a Brand Website

| Dimension | Brand Website (6.2) | Digital Product Sales Page (this lesson) |
|---|---|---|
| Core Goal | Build awareness and trust, diverse showcase | Single focus, drive purchase/conversion |
| Page Structure | Relatively open, varied sections | Highly structured, follows conversion logic |
| Success Criterion | Visitor engagement, brand recognition | Visitor completes a purchase/lead-capture action |

> Instructor note: Emphasize that "it's still building a page with Claude Code, but a sales page requires a much stronger sense of marketing structure — this is the core added value of this lesson compared to 6.2."

### II. The Classic Conversion Structure for Digital Product Sales Pages

Give learners a ready-to-use section framework (a practical implementation of the classic AIDA/PAS marketing structure):

```
1. Hero hook: A one-sentence pain point/value proposition + core CTA button
2. Pain-point resonance: Describe the specific struggles the target user currently faces
3. Solution introduction: How this digital product solves the pain points above
4. Product content/feature breakdown: What's specifically included (chapters/modules/feature list)
5. Differentiated advantages: Why choose this over other options
6. Social proof: User reviews, case studies, data (if available)
7. Pricing and purchase plans: Clear pricing display, including a purchase button
8. FAQ: Preemptively resolve purchase concerns
9. Final call-to-action: Restate the value proposition + CTA button
```

### III. Hands-On: From Brief to Sales Page Development

**Step 1: Clarify the Core Selling Point and Pain Points (Reuse the 6.1 Brief)**

```
> Based on @docs/LandingPageBrief.md, please help me build a digital product sales page
> organized into nine sections: "hero hook, pain-point resonance, solution, feature
> breakdown, differentiated advantages, social proof, pricing plans, FAQ, final CTA,"
> following the visual guidelines in @docs/Visual-Design-System.md.
```

**Step 2: Focus on Polishing the Hero Section and CTA (The Key Conversion Zone)**

- The hero section is the key factor in whether a visitor continues scrolling, and is worth polishing repeatedly on its own;

```
> For the hero section, please give me 3 different headline + subheadline combinations
> from different angles, each emphasizing one of "pain-point impact," "value promise,"
> and "curiosity hook" — I'll pick one.
```

**Step 3: Clarity Check for the Pricing Section**

- Pricing information must be clear and unambiguous. Check that the following are explicitly listed: price, what's included, any limited-time discount, and refund policy (if applicable);

```
> Please check whether the pricing section's information is complete and clear —
> can a first-time visitor accurately understand exactly how much they'll pay and
> what they'll get?
```

**Step 4: FAQ Section — Anticipating Purchase Concerns**

```
> Based on this product's characteristics, please help me list 5 concerns a user might
> have before purchasing, and provide clear, persuasive answers to use as the FAQ
> section content.
```

### IV. Compliance and Integrity Reminder (Important)

- Sales page copy can easily slide toward exaggerated claims. Always hold the line of "real and verifiable": don't fabricate user reviews, don't exaggerate outcome promises, and keep pricing and discount information accurate and truthful;
- Marketing techniques such as "limited-time offers," countdowns, or "low stock" indicators should only be used when based on real circumstances — avoid creating false urgency, which can damage user trust and may even carry consumer-protection regulatory risk.

### V. Conversion Elements Self-Check Checklist

- [ ] Within 3 seconds of viewing the hero section, can a visitor understand "what this is and what they'll get";
- [ ] At least 2–3 clear CTA buttons exist (not only placed at the very bottom of the page);
- [ ] Pricing information is clear and unambiguous;
- [ ] The FAQ covers realistic, plausible purchase concerns;
- [ ] All promotional content is truthful and credible, with no exaggeration or fabrication.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Does a sales page have to follow the order of these nine sections?**
A: This is a classic, effective default order, and it can be adjusted based on the product's characteristics (for example, if the product itself is already well-known, the pain-point resonance section can be simplified). It's recommended to keep the core logic of "first build agreement on the need, then introduce the solution, and finally drive conversion."

**Q: What if I don't have enough data/user reviews?**
A: It's normal for a new product to lack case studies — you can build trust instead through the "creator's background/professional credentials." Never fabricate fake reviews just because you lack real case studies.
