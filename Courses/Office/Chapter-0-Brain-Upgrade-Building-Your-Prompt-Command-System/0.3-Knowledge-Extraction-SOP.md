## Course Outline

### I. Why Knowledge Extraction Is Worth Practicing on Its Own

- A large amount of time in daily work is spent "reading through a pile of material and finding the key points" — long meeting minutes, long reports, endless email threads, past chat logs;
- Simply telling AI to "summarize this" often produces a vague, generic overview that misses the points that actually matter — for the same reason discussed in 0.1: the task hasn't been structurally defined;
- What knowledge extraction needs to answer is not "what did this piece of content say," but "what information here is useful for my next decision or action."

### II. The Knowledge Extraction SOP: A Four-Step Method

```
Step 1: Clarify the purpose of the extraction
  —— What follow-up decision or action am I extracting this content for?
Step 2: Set the extraction dimensions
  —— What angle should the material be organized by? (Timeline? Priority level?
      Action items vs. background information?)
Step 3: Perform the extraction
  —— Have Claude process the source content according to the purpose and dimensions you set
Step 4: Verify and check for gaps
  —— Spot-check whether key information has been missed or misrepresented; clearly
      flag any parts that can't be extracted because the source content isn't available
```

### III. Hands-on: Walking Through the Full Knowledge Extraction SOP

Using "a lengthy meeting record" as an example:

**Step 1: Clarify the purpose**

```
I'm extracting this meeting record in order to quickly know "what things I need
to follow up on this week"
```

**Step 2: Set the dimensions**

```
Organize into three categories: "action items, questions requiring my decision,
and background information for reference only"
```

**Step 3: Perform the extraction (structured Prompt)**

```
Goal: Extract from this meeting record the information I need to follow up on
Context: I am the team member responsible for execution; I don't need to understand
  every detail of the discussion, only the content relevant to action
Format: List in three categories — "action items (including owner and deadline, if
  mentioned), questions requiring my decision, and background information for
  reference only"
Constraints: Do not omit any content that mentions a specific date or a
  responsible person
```

**Step 4: Verify and check for gaps**

```
Please confirm: do all the specific dates and names mentioned in this meeting
record appear in your extracted result? If anything is missing, please add it.
```

### IV. Common Pitfalls in Knowledge Extraction

- **Pitfall 1: Just asking for a "summary" without setting a purpose** — a summary with no purpose tends to be comprehensive but pointless; always think through "who is this extraction for, and what will it be used for" first;
- **Pitfall 2: Fully trusting the extracted result without verification** — AI's extraction is generated based on its understanding, so important information (dates, amounts, responsible parties) must be manually verified, especially in scenarios that will feed into key decisions;
- **Pitfall 3: Feeding in too much raw material at once** — because of how context processing capacity works, too much or overly mixed material dilutes extraction quality; it's recommended to focus on material for a single topic and process it in batches.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: If the material is extremely long (say, a document of tens of thousands of characters), can it be processed all at once?**
A: It depends on the specific length and complexity. When the material is too long, it's recommended to process it in segments first, then run a second round of consolidation across the segment-level extraction results, rather than expecting a single pass to perfectly handle an extremely large amount of material.

**Q: What if the extracted content feels like "something's missing"?**
A: This is exactly the purpose of Step 4, "Verify and check for gaps" — proactively asking "was any information of type XX missed" is more reliable than passively waiting for the AI to fill in the gaps on its own.
