# 3.3 | Market Research: Finding Content Demand and Opportunities

## Learning Objectives

- Master the basic methodology for doing content market research with Claude Code;
- Be able to produce insights around a niche about "what users are asking, what content is missing";
- Complete a market research report containing at least 3 actionable topic directions;
- Understand information verification and the boundaries of compliant use during the research process.

## Suggested Duration

30 minutes

## Prerequisites

- Have completed 3.1 and 3.2, and already have a clear account positioning and content/visual guidelines to use as a reference frame for the research direction.

## Course Outline

### I. What Content Creators Are Actually Looking for When They Do Market Research

This isn't market research in the academic sense — it's about answering three concrete questions:

1. **What does my audience care about** (trending topics, pain points);
2. **What's missing from existing content** (supply gaps — angles others haven't covered thoroughly, or haven't covered at all);
3. **What kinds of expression are more likely to get noticed** (headline structure, content format preferences).

### II. Research Information Sources and Methods

Introduce learners to a few common types of research inputs, and explain how Claude Code can assist with processing each:

| Information Source | Purpose | How Claude Code Helps |
|---|---|---|
| Search engines/news sites | Understand current trending topics and industry developments | Web search + summarization |
| Social platform comment/Q&A sections | Discover real user pain points and frequently asked questions | Organize and categorize authentic user voices, extract common patterns |
| Historical content from benchmark accounts in the same niche | Understand existing content coverage | Batch summarization, identify content gaps |
| Your own historical data (backend comments/DMs) | Understand your own audience's specific needs | Structured organization, sentiment/topic categorization |

> Instructor note: When it comes to scraping data from third-party platforms, remind learners to comply with platform terms of use, prioritize data/publicly visible information officially opened by the platform, and avoid large-scale scraping that circumvents restrictions.

### III. Hands-On: A Complete Research Workflow Walkthrough

Using the learner's own niche as an example, demonstrate live:

**Step 1: Collecting Trends and Pain Points**

```
> Please search for the most discussed topics related to "[your niche keyword]" over the past month, and summarize the 5 questions users ask most often
```

**Step 2: Organizing into a Structured List**

```
> Please organize the information you just found into a table, using two dimensions: "topic popularity" and "whether content supply is sufficient"
```

**Step 3: Identifying Content Gaps/Opportunities**

```
> Based on this table, which topics are opportunities where "popularity is high but existing content isn't good enough/deep enough"? Please give your reasoning
```

**Step 4: Turning Opportunities into Concrete Topics**

```
> Please turn each of the 3 opportunities we found into a concrete content topic (headline direction + core angle + suitable content format)
```

### IV. Compiling the Research Report

Compile everything into `docs/market-research-report-[date].md`. Suggested structure:

```markdown
# Market Research Report

## Research Scope and Timeframe
## Summary of Trending Topics
## High-Frequency User Questions
## Inventory of Existing Content Supply
## Content Opportunities (at least 3)
## Recommended Topic List
```

### V. Information Verification and Compliance Reminders

- When information obtained through AI web search involves specific data/facts, cross-verify the source — don't treat the AI's summary as the final source of truth;
- When researching other people's content, use it to "discover gaps and gather inspiration" — your output should be your own original angle, avoiding directly copying others' views or expression;
- When analyzing specific individuals/accounts, maintain objective, neutral statements — no personal attacks or malicious speculation about motives.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Will the topics I find through research overlap with other people's?**
A: Research is inherently about observing "what everyone is paying attention to" — overlap is normal. The key to differentiation is your unique perspective and expression (echoing the Personal Content Context from 3.1), not exclusive ownership of the topic itself.

**Q: How thorough does the research need to be?**
A: The minimum bar is "being able to produce 3 or more concrete, actionable topics." Don't aim to cover the entire niche — focus on the scope relevant to your account positioning. Research itself can also be an ongoing, lightweight daily habit rather than a one-time massive project.
