# 3.1 | Building Your Personal Content Context

## Learning Objectives

- Understand the value of a "Personal Content Context": making AI write content that sounds "like you," not "like anyone";
- Master the four core modules a content context should include: persona, tone, content history, and do-not list;
- Complete a reusable Personal Content Context document and integrate it into `CLAUDE.md` / a Claude Project.

## Suggested Duration

30 minutes

## Prerequisites

- Have completed 2.2 (CLAUDE.md) and 2.3 (Claude Projects) — this lesson is a concrete application of both in a content-creation setting.

## Course Outline

### I. Why You Need a "Personal Content Context"

- Pain point: Asking AI to "write a Xiaohongshu post" with no groundwork typically produces content in a "generic, mass-market style" that lacks distinctiveness and reads "like AI wrote it";
- Solution: Explicitly tell the AI about your unique voice, positions, and content history, so it has "material" to imitate and build on, instead of generating from nothing;
- Analogy: If you hire a new ghostwriter and give them only a one-line brief, they can only produce a "textbook answer." If instead you show them your past 20 articles and tell them your catchphrases and things to avoid, they can write something that sounds "like you."

### II. The Four Modules of a Personal Content Context

**1. Persona**
- Who you are: identity, professional background, account positioning;
- Who your readers are: target audience profile;
- The keywords you want to be remembered by (e.g., "down-to-earth," "sharp," "warm").

**2. Tone & Voice**
- Habitual sentence patterns and catchphrases;
- Habits around using emojis and exclamation points;
- Preference for long vs. short sentences, paragraph rhythm;
- Explicit counter-examples of "how you don't speak" (e.g., "never open with 'hey everyone'").

**3. Content History**
- Core ideas and headline style of 5–10 curated representative pieces;
- Which content performed well (this can later be reinforced with the viral-content analysis results from 3.4);
- Which content performed poorly, to avoid repeating the same mistakes.

**4. Do-Not List**
- Topics/positions that are off-limits;
- Brand- and regulation-related red lines;
- Expressions that previously caused controversy or performed poorly.

### III. Hands-On: Using Claude Code to Help Organize Your Content Context

Organizing your content context can itself be accelerated with Claude Code, rather than being written entirely by hand:

1. Put the 5–10 pieces of historical content you've prepared into the project's `docs/content-history/` folder;
2. In Claude Code, enter:

```
> Please read all the articles in docs/content-history/ and help me summarize:
> 1. The sentence patterns and catchphrases I commonly use
> 2. The overall tone characteristics of my writing
> 3. The topics and positions I tend to discuss
> Please present this as a bulleted list so I can review and revise it
```

3. Compare against the AI's summary and confirm/correct it point by point — this step is critical; the AI's summary is a draft, and your judgment is the final standard;
4. Organize the confirmed content into the four modules and save it as `docs/personal-content-context.md`.

### IV. Integrating into Your Workflow

- Write a condensed summary of your finished content context into the project's `CLAUDE.md` (the core parts that are long-term and used every time);
- Keep the full version as a standalone reference document in the `docs/` directory, to be referenced whenever needed;
- Sync a copy into the corresponding Claude Project's knowledge base (echoing 2.3), so the same persona can be invoked in Desktop conversations as well.

### V. Validating the Effect

Test with a fresh conversation with no prior context loaded:

```
> Please write a short commentary, in my style, about "[a recent hot topic in your field]"
```

Compare the output against your persona, tone, and content-history preferences. If there's a significant gap, go back and supplement/revise your content context document.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: I don't have any content history yet — my account is brand new. What should I do?**
A: You can work backward from "the style you want to present" — describe 2–3 creators whose style you admire as a reference point for the AI (not copying their content, but describing "I want the tone to feel more like this"). As your account accumulates content, iterate and replace this with your own actual history.

**Q: How often does the content context need to be updated?**
A: It's recommended that after each batch of new content is published, you review the performance data and update "expressions that worked well" and "pitfalls to avoid" into the do-not list and content history summary, turning it into a continuously evolving living document.
